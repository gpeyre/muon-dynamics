Equal-weight particle measures. Generative modeling: D is any suitable discrepancy. For two-layer networks, u is the outer weight and v the inner weight; for training data (z_j,y_j), f(alpha) = (1/m) sum_j loss(h_alpha(z_j),y_j). Single-cell example: learn cell-fate landscapes and population trajectories from snapshots. This is a modeling perspective, not a universal description of biology. Reference: Huizing, Samaran et al., STORIES, Nature Methods 23, 522-531 (2026), doi:10.1038/s41592-025-02855-4. Experiment: W2 descent of squared MMD for the energy-distance kernel -|x-y|, numerically smoothed as -sqrt(|x-y|^2 + 0.001^2). Target: 2,048 fixed samples of an equal mixture of two Gaussians. Contours show the population mixture density. Explicit Euler, dt=0.04, T=16; 101 saved frames, linearly interpolated for playback. Initial particles are red, target contours blue, and moving particles interpolate from red to blue. Changing n chooses a separately computed interacting-particle system, not a subset of the same simulated flow. Initial clouds share a fixed nested random sample.
